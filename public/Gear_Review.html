The Ultimate Guide to Juggling Gear

The difference between catching and dropping the club often comes down to the equipment and its quality. from the handles flex to how heavy it is. Discover the science behind the perfect club for you.

Section 1: Choosing Your Club Size

Picking the correct club size for you is one of the most difficult and crucial decisions for anyone looking to juggle. A club that's too large can make you clumsy, and it could hit your arms more easily, while a club that's too small might not be as visible to the crowd and could look bad with your body proportions when juggling. By matching your club's width to your height and wingspan, you make sure that the equipment feels more like an extension of your body instead of a toy, allowing for more fluent and natural movement. with a quicker skill progression

95mm (Standard):

recomented for most people sized (5’6” to 6’0”). It provides a good balance between visual balance and comfort, and is the most common choice.

85mm (Small):

more recommended for shorter people sized under 5’7” or for those with smaller frames and armspan, because it looks and feels more scaled to your physique.

105mm (Stage/Pro):

Designed for taller jugglers (over 6’) or for people performing in front of a crowd that wants a bigger and more dramatic visual impact for your audience.

Section 2: The "Handle" Debate: Taped vs. Standard

The handle is one of the most importent part of your club as its your primary point of contact with it, and the material you pick often directly affects your comfort and control of the club. Beginners often struggle with something called ‘sting,’ the sharp pain that you feel when catching the fast-spinning club. Choosing the correct handle style can minimise this pain and help you learn for longer. Whether you prefer the soft feeling of tape or the precision of the bair plastic, your grip style should reflect your current skill level and your response to your environment.

Taped Handles (The Beginner Choice):

These handles are wrapped in vinyl tape. Or you can tape them yourself.

Pros: much softer on the hands, this reduces the pain when you are learning to catch or for people who practice or perform for hours on end

Cons: require maintenance like taking off dirty or when it loses its grip, and applying a new coating of the tape

Standard Handles (The Pro Choice):

These handles are made of smooth, smooth plastic (HDPE). All clubs come with this.

Pros: There is zero maintenance, and they have more “accuracy” as the handle is a lot stiffer and you have more control over it during the release of the club.

Cons: They are way harder on your hands, especially for beginners, and can get slippery if your hands get sweaty. This happens a lot more than you think, especially when you have a lot of ‘handle sting’.

Section 3: Weight and Performance

Consistency is the key to practising and mastering arts; this involves the cascade. learn why the weight of the club matters and how it can affect your wrists while you're trying to protect your throws.

Indoor Juggling:

Lighter clubs (220g) are way easier on your arms for long practice sessions, but could affect your control and feel.

Outdoor Juggling:

Heavier clubs (245g+) are way better if you have the strength in your wrists or if you're experienced, as you have alot more control over the club with spin and height, it also just feels better and won’t blow around in the wind as much.

Pro Tip: if you’re buying a club as a gift for someone, the “safe bet” is to get them a 95mm club with solid colours and a taped handle. It's the universal standard!
