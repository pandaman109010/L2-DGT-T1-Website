The Ultimate Guide to Juggling Gear

The difference between a drop and a catch often comes down to your equipment. From handle flex to weight distribution, discover the science behind the perfect club.

Section 1: Choosing Your Club Size

Selecting the correct club size is the most critical decision for any juggler. A club that is too large can feel cumbersome and collide with your arms, while a club that is too small might lack the visual presence needed for a clean pattern. By matching the club’s width to your physical height and wingspan, you ensure that the equipment feels like an extension of your body, allowing for more natural movement and quicker skill progression.

95mm (Standard):

Recommended for most people (5'6” to 6'0"). It provides a great visual balance and is the most common choice.

85mm (Small):

Best for shorter individuals (under 5'7") or those with smaller frames, as it feels more scaled to your physique.

105mm (Stage/Pro):

Designed for tall jugglers (over 6') or performers who want a massive visual impact for a stage audience.

Section 2: The "Handle" Debate: Taped vs. Standard

The handle is your primary point of contact with the club, and the material you choose directly impacts your comfort and control. Beginners often struggle with 'sting'—the sharp pain felt when catching a fast-spinning club. Choosing the right handle finish can minimize this discomfort and help you practice for longer periods without fatigue. Whether you prefer the soft cushion of tape or the sleek precision of plastic, your grip style should reflect your current skill level and environment.

Taped Handles (The Beginner Choice):

These handles are wrapped in white vinyl tape.

Pros: Much softer on the hands, which reduces pain when you are first learning to catch.

Cons: Require maintenance; the tape may need replacing as it gets dirty or loses its grip.

Standard Handles (The Pro Choice):

These handles are made of a smooth, durable material.

Pros: Zero maintenance and more "accuracy" because the handle is stiffer and doesn't flex as much during the release.

Cons: Harder on the hands for beginners and can feel slippery if your hands get sweaty.

Section 3: Weight and Performance

Consistency is key to mastering the cascade. Learn why professional standards matter and how to choose a weight that protects your wrists while perfecting your throws.

Indoor Juggling:

Lighter clubs (220g+) are easier on your arms for long practice sessions.

Outdoor Juggling:

Heavier clubs (245g+) are better if you're practicing at the park, as they aren't pushed around by the wind as easily.

Pro Tip: If you're buying clubs as a gift, the "Safe Bet" is always a 95mm decorated club with a long handle. It’s the universal standard!
